# Acme Corp Expense Policy

Document Type: Expense Policy
Document Reference: FIN-2026-EXP
Department: Finance
Effective Date: 1 January 2026
Owner: Financial Controller

This policy governs what Acme Corp will reimburse, the limits that apply,
and how claims are submitted. It applies to all employees, contractors
engaged directly by Acme Corp, and to candidates attending interviews at
the company's request.

Claims are processed under reference code FIN-2026-EXP. Quote this code
in any correspondence with the Finance team about a reimbursement.

## 1. Principles

Three tests decide whether an expense is reimbursable. The expense must
be incurred wholly and necessarily in the course of company business, it
must be reasonable in the circumstances, and it must be supported by
evidence.

An expense that passes all three tests is reimbursed even if it is not
listed in this document. An expense that fails any of them is not
reimbursed even if it is listed.

Employees should not be out of pocket for legitimate business costs, and
should not use company funds for personal benefit. Where a claim sits in
the grey area between the two, discuss it with the Finance team before
incurring the cost rather than after.

## 2. Travel Expenses

### 2.1 Meals

Employees may expense meals during business travel up to 60 USD per day.
The daily cap covers all meals and non-alcoholic drinks taken during the
travel day. It is a cap and not an allowance: only what is actually spent
may be claimed.

Where a meal is provided as part of a conference fee or by a client, the
daily cap is reduced proportionately. Breakfast is treated as 25 per cent
of the cap, lunch as 30 per cent, and dinner as 45 per cent.

Alcohol is not reimbursable except where it forms part of a client
entertainment event approved in advance under section 4.

#### 2.1.1 Meals Without Overnight Travel

A meal taken during a day trip is reimbursable only where the employee is
away from their normal place of work for more than 8 hours. The cap in
that case is 25 USD.

### 2.2 Accommodation

Hotel accommodation is capped at 250 USD per night in major cities and
150 USD per night elsewhere. Major cities are London, New York, San
Francisco, Tokyo, Singapore, Zurich, Paris and Sydney. Any other location
falls under the standard cap.

The cap covers the room rate and applicable taxes. It does not cover
minibar charges, in-room entertainment, laundry on stays of fewer than
five nights, or spa and gym fees.

Where no room is available within the cap, the employee should book the
nearest available option and note the reason on the claim. Finance will
approve a reasonable overage supported by a note; it will not approve an
unexplained one.

#### 2.2.1 Staying With Friends or Family

An employee who stays privately instead of in a hotel may claim a flat
30 USD per night in lieu of accommodation, without receipts. This is
capped at 5 nights per trip.

### 2.3 Mileage

Employees using a private vehicle for business travel may claim mileage
at 0.45 GBP per mile for the first 10,000 miles in a tax year and
0.25 GBP per mile thereafter.

The mileage calculation is: miles driven multiplied by the applicable
rate. A journey of 120 miles within the first 10,000 therefore reimburses
120 x 0.45.

Mileage is claimed for the business journey only. Where a journey starts
or ends at home, the claimable distance is the lesser of the actual
distance and the distance from the normal place of work.

A passenger supplement of 0.05 GBP per mile per colleague may be claimed
where colleagues travel in the same vehicle for the same business
purpose.

### 2.4 Public Transport

Rail, bus, tram and underground fares are reimbursed in full at standard
class. First class rail is reimbursable only where the ticket costs no
more than the standard class fare for the same journey, which can happen
with advance purchase.

Taxis and ride-hailing services are reimbursable where public transport
is unavailable, where the journey is between 22:00 and 06:00, where the
employee is carrying equipment, or where a disability or injury makes
public transport impractical.

### 2.5 Air Travel

Air travel is governed by the Travel Booking Policy. Expenses arising
from air travel — baggage fees, seat selection on flights over 6 hours,
airport transfers — are reimbursable under this policy.

Airport lounge access is not reimbursable except for delays exceeding
3 hours where no alternative is available.

## 3. Reimbursement

### 3.1 Receipts

Receipts are required for every expense above 25 USD. Below that
threshold a description of the expense is sufficient, though a receipt is
always preferred.

A valid receipt shows the vendor, the date, the amount, and what was
purchased. A card statement line is not a receipt. A photograph of a
paper receipt is acceptable.

### 3.2 Deadlines

Expense reports must be filed within 30 days of the trip ending, or
within 30 days of the expense being incurred where no trip is involved.

Claims submitted between 30 and 60 days require a written explanation and
manager approval. Claims submitted more than 60 days after the event are
not reimbursed except in exceptional circumstances approved by the
Financial Controller.

### 3.3 Approval Thresholds

| Claim total | Approver |
|---|---|
| Up to 250 GBP | Line manager |
| 250 to 1,000 GBP | Department director |
| 1,000 to 5,000 GBP | Financial Controller |
| Above 5,000 GBP | Chief Financial Officer |

An employee may not approve their own claim, nor a claim from which they
personally benefit.

### 3.4 Payment

Approved claims are paid with the next payroll run. Payroll closes on the
20th of each month. A claim approved after that date is paid in the
following month.

Payment is made to the bank account held for salary. Employees cannot
nominate a different account for expenses.

### 3.5 Currency

Expenses incurred in a foreign currency are converted at the rate on the
date of the transaction, using the rate shown on the employee's card
statement where the card was used, or the published mid-market rate
otherwise.

Currency conversion fees charged by a personal card provider are
reimbursable and should be claimed as a separate line.

## 4. Client Entertainment

Client entertainment requires approval in advance from a department
director. The request must name the client, the attendees, the business
purpose and the expected cost.

The limit is 75 GBP per head including alcohol. Entertainment of public
officials is prohibited without prior legal review, regardless of value.

Gifts to clients are limited to 50 GBP per recipient per year and must be
recorded in the gifts register maintained by Legal.

## 5. Non-Reimbursable Items

Personal entertainment, minibar charges and flight upgrades are not
reimbursable under any circumstances.

The following are also excluded:

- Parking and speeding fines, and any other penalty
- Loss or theft of personal property
- Personal travel insurance beyond the company policy
- Home broadband, except under the remote work allowance below
- Childcare and pet care costs incurred because of business travel
- Clothing, including business attire
- Membership of professional bodies, unless required for the role and
  approved separately by the department director

## 6. Remote Work Allowance

Employees approved for fully remote work receive a one-off 300 GBP
allowance towards a desk and chair, and 25 GBP per month towards
broadband and utilities. The monthly amount is paid through payroll and
is subject to tax.

The one-off allowance is claimed through the expense system with
receipts. Equipment purchased with it remains the property of the
employee.

## 7. Corporate Cards

Corporate cards are issued to employees who travel more than four times
a year, at the discretion of the Financial Controller.

Card transactions must be reconciled in the expense system within 15 days
of the statement date. Unreconciled transactions above 100 GBP may be
recovered from salary after two reminders.

The card must not be used for personal expenditure, even where the
employee intends to repay it.

## 8. Audit and Misuse

Finance samples 5 per cent of claims each month for audit, and audits all
claims above 2,000 GBP.

Deliberate submission of a false or inflated claim is gross misconduct
and may result in summary dismissal and referral to the police. Repeated
careless errors are handled under the disciplinary procedure.
