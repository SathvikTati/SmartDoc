ACME CORP PAYROLL AND OVERTIME GUIDE

Document Reference: PAY-2026-OT
Department: Finance, Payroll
Effective Date: 1 January 2026

This guide explains how pay is calculated for hours worked beyond the
standard working week, for weekend and public holiday work, and for shift
patterns. It applies to all employees whose contract states an hourly
rate or an annual salary with an entitlement to overtime.

1. WHAT COUNTS AS OVERTIME

Overtime is the amount of time an employee works beyond normal working
hours. The standard working week is 37.5 hours. Any hour worked beyond
37.5 in a single week is overtime.

Overtime hours have to be approved by your respective Managers with
complete justifications at least one hour before overtime is required.
Overtime worked without prior approval is not paid, though a manager may
approve it retrospectively where the circumstances made prior approval
impossible.

Overtime hours will be tracked through the Time and Attendance module.
Hours not recorded in the module by the end of the pay period are not
paid.

2. THE BASIC CALCULATION

The basic calculation is Overtime pay = Hourly pay rate x 1.5 x overtime
hours worked.

For a salaried employee the hourly pay rate is derived from the annual
salary. The formula is annual salary divided by 1950. The figure 1950 is
37.5 hours multiplied by 52 weeks.

Worked example. An employee on an annual salary of 39000 has an hourly
rate of 39000 / 1950, which is 20. If that employee works 6 approved
overtime hours in a week, the overtime pay is 20 x 1.5 x 6.

3. ENHANCED RATES

Not all overtime is paid at the same multiplier.

Weekday overtime, worked Monday to Friday, is paid at 1.5 times the
hourly rate.

Saturday overtime is paid at 1.5 times the hourly rate for the first four
hours and 2 times the hourly rate thereafter.

Sunday overtime is paid at 2 times the hourly rate for all hours.

Public holiday overtime is paid at 2.5 times the hourly rate. There are
8 public holidays in the standard calendar.

Night work, defined as hours worked between 23:00 and 06:00, attracts an
additional night allowance of 4.20 per hour on top of the applicable
overtime multiplier.

4. COMPENSATORY TIME OFF

Compensatory off will be given to employees for having worked on a
designated weekend or a public holiday. Compensatory time is granted at
the same multiplier as the pay would have been. An employee who works
4 hours on a Sunday may take 8 hours of compensatory leave instead of
being paid.

Compensatory time must be taken within 90 days of being earned. Time not
taken within that window is paid at the applicable overtime rate in the
next payroll run.

An employee may choose pay or compensatory time but not a mixture of the
two for the same period of work.

5. CAPS AND WORKING TIME LIMITS

No employee may work more than 48 hours a week averaged over a 17 week
reference period, unless they have signed an individual opt-out.

The maximum overtime payable in a single month is 40 hours. Hours beyond
that are converted to compensatory time.

An employee must have at least 11 consecutive hours of rest between
shifts, and at least one full day off in every seven.

6. SHIFT ALLOWANCES

Employees working a rotating shift pattern receive a shift allowance
calculated as a percentage of basic salary.

Two-shift pattern, covering 06:00 to 22:00, attracts 12 per cent.

Three-shift pattern, covering the full 24 hours, attracts 20 per cent.

On-call standby, where the employee is not working but must be reachable
and able to attend within one hour, is paid at 95 per hour of standby.
A call-out during standby is paid at the applicable overtime rate with a
minimum of 3 hours.

7. PART-TIME EMPLOYEES

A part-time employee works overtime only once they exceed 37.5 hours in a
week, not once they exceed their contracted hours. Hours worked between
their contracted hours and 37.5 are paid at the basic hourly rate.

8. SALARY BANDS

The current bands, in thousands, are as follows. Band 1 runs from 28 to
36. Band 2 runs from 34 to 46. Band 3 runs from 44 to 62. Band 4 runs
from 58 to 82. Band 5 runs from 78 to 110.

Bands overlap deliberately. Position within a band reflects experience
and sustained performance rather than time served.

9. DEDUCTIONS AND STATUTORY PAYMENTS

Payroll deducts income tax, national insurance, pension contributions and
any salary sacrifice arrangements before net pay is calculated.

Statutory sick pay begins on the fourth consecutive day of absence, once
company sick pay under the HR Policy is exhausted.

Student loan repayments are deducted at the plan rate notified by HMRC.

10. PAYROLL CALENDAR

Payroll closes on the 20th of each month. Payment is made on the last
working day of the month. Overtime worked after the 20th is paid in the
following month.

Payslips are published in the People Portal on the day of payment and are
retained there for 7 years.

11. QUERIES

Payroll queries should be raised with payroll@acme.example within 30 days
of the payslip date, quoting PAY-2026-OT. Errors identified after 90 days
may be corrected but will not attract interest or compensation.
