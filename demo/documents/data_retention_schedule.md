# Acme Corp Data Retention Schedule

Document Type: Records Schedule
Document Reference: DR-SCH-2026
Department: Legal and Compliance
Effective Date: 1 January 2026
Owner: Data Protection Officer

This schedule states how long each category of record is kept and what
happens at the end of the retention period. It is the authoritative
source for retention questions. Where another document states a different
period, this schedule prevails.

Retention periods run from the trigger event named in each entry, not
from the date the record was created.

## 1. Principles

Records are kept for as long as there is a lawful basis and a business
need, and no longer. Keeping a record beyond its period is a compliance
failure in the same way that destroying one early is.

Four rules govern every entry below.

Retention is a maximum, not a target. Where a record is no longer needed
before the period expires, it may be destroyed earlier unless a minimum
is stated.

A legal hold overrides everything in this schedule. Where Legal has
issued a hold, affected records are preserved regardless of their normal
period, until the hold is lifted in writing.

Destruction must be irreversible and must be logged. The log records what
was destroyed, when, under which entry, and by whom.

Backups are covered by the backup rotation in section 6 rather than by
the periods below.

## 2. Employee Records

| Record | Retention | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Recruitment records, unsuccessful candidates | 12 months | Decision date |
| Recruitment records, successful candidates | 6 years | End of employment |
| Employment contract and variations | 6 years | End of employment |
| Payroll records | 7 years | End of tax year |
| Pension records | 12 years | End of employment |
| Performance reviews | 3 years | Review date |
| Disciplinary records, warning upheld | 2 years | Date of warning |
| Disciplinary records, dismissal | 6 years | End of employment |
| Grievance records | 6 years | Resolution date |
| Sickness absence records | 3 years | End of absence |
| Right to work documentation | 2 years | End of employment |
| Training and certification records | 6 years | End of employment |

Payslips are published in the People Portal and retained there for
7 years, consistent with the payroll entry above.

## 3. Financial Records

| Record | Retention | Trigger |

| Statutory accounts | Permanent | — |
| General ledger | 7 years | End of financial year |
| Invoices, sales and purchase | 7 years | End of financial year |
| Expense claims and receipts | 7 years | End of financial year |
| Bank statements | 7 years | End of financial year |

| Corporate card statements | 7 years | Statement date |

## 4. Customer and Commercial Records

| Record | Retention | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Customer contracts | 7 years | End of contract |
| Customer contracts under seal | 13 years | End of contract |
| Support tickets | 3 years | Ticket closure |
| Customer personal data, active account | Duration of account | — |
| Customer personal data, closed account | 30 days | Account closure |
| Marketing consent records | 6 years | Consent withdrawal |
| Tender and bid documents, unsuccessful | 2 years | Decision date |

Customer personal data on a closed account is deleted after 30 days
except where an entry above requires it to be kept — a paid invoice, for
example, remains under the financial records schedule for 7 years.

## 5. Security and Systems Records

| Record | Retention | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Authentication logs | 12 months | Event date |
| Network traffic logs | 90 days | Event date |
| Application audit logs | 12 months | Event date |
| Physical access records | 12 months | Event date |
| CCTV footage | 31 days | Recording date |
| Security incident reports | 6 years | Incident closure |
| Vulnerability scan results | 24 months | Scan date |
| Penetration test reports | 6 years | Report date |

Retention of security logs is also governed by control SEC-8830 in the
Information Security Policy. Where the two differ, the longer period
applies.

## 6. Backups

Backups follow a rotation independent of the schedules above. Daily
backups are kept for 30 days, weekly backups for 13 weeks, and monthly
backups for 12 months.

A record deleted under this schedule may persist in a backup until the
rotation expires. This is accepted: restoring a backup solely to delete a
single record is disproportionate. Backups are not searched in response
to an access request for this reason.

## 7. Governance Records

| Record | Retention | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Board minutes | Permanent | — |
| Statutory registers | Permanent | — |
| Policy documents, superseded versions | 6 years | Supersession date |
| Data protection impact assessments | 6 years | Completion date |
| Records of processing activities | Current plus 3 years | — |
| Data subject requests and responses | 3 years | Response date |
| Personal data breach records | 6 years | Breach date |

Superseded policies are retained for 6 years so that it is possible to
establish which terms applied at a given date. This matters when two
versions of a policy state different figures.

## 8. Disposal

### 8.1 Electronic Records

Electronic records are deleted through the application that holds them,
which writes an entry to the destruction log automatically.

Records held outside a managed application must be deleted manually and
logged by hand. This is one reason local copies are discouraged under the
IT Acceptable Use Policy.

### 8.2 Physical Records

Paper records are destroyed by cross-cut shredding on site, or by an
accredited destruction contractor who provides a certificate.

Storage media that has held anything classified Internal or above is
destroyed physically rather than wiped.

## 9. Review

This schedule is reviewed annually by the Data Protection Officer and
approved by the board. Changes take effect from the date of approval and
are not applied retrospectively to records already destroyed.
